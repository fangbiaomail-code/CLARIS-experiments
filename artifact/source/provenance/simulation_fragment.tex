\subsection{A public simulator for the concrete proof profiles}\label{app:public-simulation}

We construct one classical online simulator for the ordinary profile $G$ and
the corrected ring profile $Q$.  The complete statement includes the public
circuit input, context, proof kind, and parameter profile.  All SHAKE calls
share the namespace controller defined above, including adversarial calls on
raw byte strings.  Each complete input determines one consistent XOF stream.
The simulator programs only the final, stage-5 challenge; stages
$1,3,4,6,7,8,9$ remain ordinary random oracles.  This explicit public algorithm
is also defined on false statements, as required after tuple replacement in
the privacy proof.

\paragraph{Compressed-opening sampling.}
Both profiles use $\lambda=256$, $\tau=22$ punctures, and a shared binary heap
with $61\,440$ leaves.  The first $250$ challenge bits select eight $12$-bit
and fourteen $11$-bit indices; the last six bits must be zero.  Let
$A(\Delta)$ be the union of the selected leaves' paths to the root.  The
compressed opening uses $K(\Delta)=|A(\Delta)|-43$ frontier seeds.  Define
$\mathsf{Good}(\Delta)$ by the six zero bits and $K(\Delta)\le245$.
This predicate depends on the public topology and challenge, not the tree
seeds.  Write $p_{\rm good}=\Pr[\mathsf{Good}(\Delta)]$ for uniform
$\Delta\in\{0,1\}^{256}$.

For node $v$, let $a_i(v)$ count the leaves of logical vector $i$ below $v$,
and let $L_i\in\{2048,4096\}$ be its size.  Independence of the disjoint
challenge blocks gives
\[
 \mathbb E K=\sum_v\left[1-\prod_{i=1}^{22}
          \left(1-\frac{a_i(v)}{L_i}\right)\right]-43
          =232.66171331741478\ldots .
\]
The source package computes this sum with exact rational arithmetic using
the implementation's leaf mapping.  Since $K$ is an integer, Markov's
inequality and independence of the six remaining bits yield
\[
 p_{\rm good}\ge\frac{1}{64}
       \left(1-\frac{\mathbb E K}{246}\right)>\frac1{1181}.
\]
We cap the simulator at $T=2^{20}$ trials, a prefix of the implemented
$2^{32}$-counter search.  Its failure probability is at most
$(1-p_{\rm good})^T\le e^{-T/1181}<2^{-1280}$.  The first-success trial count
has expectation below $1181$; keeping the final four-output batch adds at
most three outputs.

\paragraph{Simulator.}
On a well-formed statement $x$, $\mathsf{Sim}_T$ performs the following steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sample independent uniform challenges in batches of four until the
first good value, or abort at $T$.  Let $c$ be its index.  Retain every earlier
value and the complete final batch, including values after $c$.
\item Sample a uniform root seed and IV.  Generate a genuine compressed
commitment and its opening at $\Delta_c$.  Independently sample the transmitted
VOLE correction $C$ uniformly at its exact length.  Reconstruct the receiver
matrix $Q$ and commitment digest using the actual public verifier.
\item Compute the stage-1 statement digest and the stage-3 challenge
$\chi_1$ normally.  Sample the check proof $h\gets\{0,1\}^{272}$ and the
witness correction $d\gets\{0,1\}^{\ell}$, where $\ell$ is the witness
length.  Run the receiver's VOLE check, including its stage-9 hash, and query
stage~4 for $\chi_2$.  The latter has $104$ bytes in $G$ and $208$ in $Q$.
\item Sample one uniform field element $p_1$ for $G$, or three
$p_1,p_2,p_3$ for $Q$.  Apply $d$ to $Q$ and execute the actual constraint
verifier and QuickSilver verification arithmetic, obtaining $z$.
\item Form the exact final prefix: $\chi_2\|p_1\|z$ for $G$, or
$\chi_2\|z\|p_1\|p_2\|p_3$ for $Q$.  At each retained counter append its
four-byte little-endian encoding and the suffix byte.  If any resulting
stage-5 input is already assigned, abort without overwriting it; otherwise
assign the retained challenges at all these inputs.  Serialize the proof
with the opening, $\Delta_c$, IV, and counter $c$.
\end{enumerate}
Longer XOF outputs receive consistent fresh extensions.  The procedure is an
atomic classical proof-service call; the adversary receives neither its
private randomness nor its internal query log.

\begin{proposition}[Public extension]\label{prop:public-extension}
For every well-formed public statement, including a false statement,
$\mathsf{Sim}_T$ returns an accepting proof unless it aborts.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The compressed opening is genuine and good.  The public verifier therefore
reconstructs exactly the matrix, check digest, challenges, and QuickSilver
check used by the simulator.  Its final input is the point programmed to
$\Delta_c$.  These are all its checks.  The argument requires no witness;
acceptance on a false statement relies on the simulator's programming
capability, which an ordinary attacker does not possess.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Masking argument.}
Fix a good $\Delta$ and replace the hidden expanded VOLE stream in each
logical vector by an independent uniform row string.  Let $U_i$ be the XOR
of its streams.  The invertible map
$(U_0,\ldots,U_{21})\mapsto(U_0,U_0+U_1,\ldots,U_0+U_{21})$ shows that
$C$ is uniform and the common $U=U_0$ remains uniform given $C$ and the
opened material.  Receiver reconstruction fixes $Q$, with $Q=V+U\Delta$.

Split $U$ into witness rows, QuickSilver masks, and a $272$-bit check-mask
tail $U_T$.  The implemented check is
$h=L_{\chi_1}(U_W,U_M)+U_T$.  Thus $h$ is uniform; conditioning on it leaves
$U_W,U_M$ free.  Linearity makes the receiver's stage-9 input equal the
sender's.  The check proof is exactly $34$ bytes, not two full field elements.
For a genuine witness $w$, $d=w+U_W$ is uniform.  Conditioning on $d$ fixes
the witness rows while leaving the QuickSilver masks independent.

Let $B_j$ be the unmasked constraint coefficients.  In $G$, the proof is
$p_1=B_1+u_0$.  In $Q$, writing $q_i=v_i+\Delta u_i$ for the three receiver
mask fields, the implementation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_3&=B_3+u_2,\\
 p_2&=B_2+u_1+q_2+\Delta u_2,\\
 p_1&=B_1+u_0+q_1+\Delta u_1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This triangular map has unit diagonal for every $\Delta$, including zero.
Consequently the transmitted fields are independently uniform.  For a true
witness the highest residual coefficient vanishes, so the constant
coefficient equals the check recomputed by the verifier.  The ideal-stream
transcript is therefore exactly the simulator's transcript at fixed
$\Delta$.  No honest-prover distribution on false statements is invoked.

\paragraph{Generator assumption and shared-root reduction.}
Let $\eps_{\rm punc}^{(S)}$ denote the selective punctured-stream distinguishing
advantage across $S$ calls to this concrete generator: choose each good
$\Delta$ before its fresh tree
generation, reveal the compressed opening, all opened leaf streams, hidden leaf commitments, and aggregate commitment digest, and
compare its genuine hidden streams with independent uniform strings under
adaptive public-oracle access.  This is an explicit primitive hiding game,
independent of any circuit, witness, or proof-system ZK definition.

A multipuncture hybrid replaces the expansions of marked internal nodes
top-down.  There are $|A|-22=K+21\le266$ such nodes; treating the logical
vectors as independent-root trees would be incorrect.  Once these expansions
are randomized, hidden leaf seeds are independent.  The stage-6 output splits
into a secret $256$-bit stream key and a public $512$-bit commitment.
Replacing the $22$ secret-key streams and undoing the tree hybrids gives
\[
\eps_{\rm punc}^{(S)}\le532S\eps_{\rm tree}
                       +22S\eps_{\rm stream}+\eps_{\rm hit}.
\]
Here the PRG advantages concern the configured AES-CTR generators with their
public IV and auxiliary oracle access.  To embed a stream-PRG challenge whose
key is unknown, the reduction keeps the hidden stage-6 key as a private
opaque slot: subsequent construction needs only the supplied row stream and
public commitment.  It aborts if another call reaches that leaf-oracle input.
Before such a hit, neither the commitment nor stream reveals the independent
hidden leaf seed.  Set $H=22S$; for $q_6$ other leaf-oracle calls,
\[
 \eps_{\rm hit}\le2\left[
       \frac{\binom H2}{2^{256}}
       +\frac{Hq_6}{2^{256}-q_6}\right],\qquad q_6<2^{256}.
\]
Only the targets' own private assignments are excluded
from $q_6$; opened-leaf, adversarial, and other service calls are included.
The factor two conservatively covers both endpoints.  Known-key AES
computations continue normally: a coincidence with a challenge key is already
part of the PRG distinguishing game.  No independence of public derived
tree nodes is assumed.  Thus the hiding condition reduces to the stated
generator PRG advantages and explicit hidden-input events.  Opaque slots
are used only in this public-view PRG reduction.  The actual simulator knows
its genuine leaf keys and gives the matched extractor explicit internal logs;
that private extractor is not exposed to the ZK distinguisher.

\paragraph{Fiat--Shamir order and joint error.}
The randomized wrappers use a fresh private $64$-byte seed in stage~2.
Replacing its hidden output by a uniform root and IV costs $\eps_{\rm seed}$;
the deterministic null-seed API is outside this claim.  With $q_2$ other
stage-2 queries, one may take
$\eps_{\rm seed}\le\binom S2/2^{512}+S q_2/(2^{512}-q_2)$,
for $q_2<2^{512}$, excluding the $S$ private target assignments themselves and capping at one.
At the start of call
$i$, let $q_{a,i}^{-}$ count assigned stage-$a$ inputs.  The fresh IV gives
an old stage-3-input probability at most $q_{3,i}^{-}/2^{128}$.  Otherwise
$\chi_1$ is fresh; the corresponding stage-4 bound is
$q_{4,i}^{-}/2^{1344}$.  A fresh $\chi_2$ similarly bounds any previous
stage-5-prefix match.  Set
\[
 \eps_{{\rm fresh},i}=
 \frac{q_{3,i}^{-}}{2^{128}}+
 \frac{q_{4,i}^{-}}{2^{1344}}+
 \frac{q_{5,i}^{-}}{2^{b_4}},\qquad
 b_4=\begin{cases}832&G,\\1664&Q.\end{cases}
\]
Outside this event the real counter outputs are independent uniform values,
so they may be sampled before tree generation.  The punctured-stream hybrid
then uses a selectively chosen puncture.  Its final hash inputs are computed
from the resulting transcript; obsolete hash inputs are never retained.
Keeping all rejected trials and the final four-output batch preserves the
actual first-success distribution.  Always using counter zero would not.

The same freshness bound holds for the simulator.  Truncating both endpoint
experiments on that event, the adaptive joint simulation error for $S$ calls
is bounded by
\[
 \eta_Z\le\eps_{\rm seed}+\eps_{\rm punc}^{(S)}
       +2\sum_{i=1}^S\eps_{{\rm fresh},i}
       +S(1-p_{\rm good})^T.
\]
All adversarial and service queries are counted in one controller.  The
unchanged stages $6$--$9$ support the accompanying classical log extractor;
a programmed stage-5 point is recorded against its complete simulation
statement.  This establishes the required classical simulation interface
under the stated generator hiding condition.  It does not supply quantum
oracle programming or a QROM extractor.
